Quiet NH Foliage Run

A photography day with short hikes and almost no other people. The trick is simple: never touch the Kancamagus or Franconia Notch, and be on a trail before 9am.

My pick for this Saturday

Mount Kearsarge: the most color you can reach in under two hours

A toll road through Rollins State Park takes you to within half a mile of the summit, then a 15-minute rocky walk to a bare 360° top with a fire tower. At 2,937 ft it's the highest thing within two hours of Boston, so it turns first. Views to the Whites, Monadnock and Vermont. Arrive when the road opens and you'll have the summit nearly to yourselves for an hour.

The summit is bare granite with real wind. 65° in Warner is 50° up top. The road closes if it's icy, which won't be an issue this weekend.

Red Hill fire tower, then lunch on Squam

A 1.7-mile hike, about an hour up, to a fire tower looking over Winnipesaukee, Squam and the Ossipee range. It's the quiet neighbor of West Rattlesnake, which is where everyone else goes. Lunch at the Corner House in Center Sandwich, then the Squam Lake shoreline on the way out. This is tab B's lunch stop with a better hike and without the long leg east to Foss.

Sandwich Notch Road and Beede Falls, nothing else

Tab B without the Foss leg. A one-lane dirt road through old forest and stone walls, a 15-minute walk to a wide, low waterfall, and lunch in Center Sandwich. Everything is in the trees, so it doesn't depend on the summit color being at peak. Best if you want slow wandering and close frames rather than a view.

Sculptured Rocks and Newfound Lake

A narrow granite gorge with glacial potholes and a swimming hole, ten minutes from Wellington State Park's beach on Newfound Lake, one of the clearest lakes in the state. Very few people. Water and rock, small scenes, not vistas. Pair it with the Hebron village common and Grafton Pond for stillness.

Your trimmed route, with stops added

Up Route 16, back down Route 16, and everything worth a frame in between

Your Back Bay → Eaton → Foss → Back Bay route is 4h 41 of driving. Three short days, all under three hours of total driving

None of these touch New Hampshire's big mountains, so they peak later, roughly Oct 12–25. Pick by what you want to shoot: a hilltop view, old farms and stone walls, or water and woods close to home.

My pick

One real hike, one slow scenic drive, home by dinner

Pondicherry in morning light, breakfast in Littleton, then Route 302 through Crawford Notch with the windows down. Skip Bear Notch and Foss. It's the fewest miles for the biggest views, and it still feels like a date instead of a logistics exercise.

Why not loop home through Conway? It adds 45 minutes and puts you on Route 16 at the busiest time. Going back the way you came is quicker on a weekday.

The whole loop, split over two days, with a real sunrise and a real sunset

Stay in Jackson or North Conway. Day one is a lazy drive up with Crawford Notch and Bear Notch Road in afternoon light. Day two starts at Pondicherry for sunrise and ends on Foss Mountain at golden hour. No 5:30am alarm, no 9pm drive home, and you get the two best light windows of the trip.

The spots, in drive order
1

Pondicherry Wildlife Refuge, Jefferson

3h 00 from Back Bay1.5 mi flat, each waySunrise is the shot

An old rail bed walks you to Cherry Pond, where the whole Presidential Range sits reflected in still water. On a cold clear morning there is fog on the pond until about 8:30. Almost nobody comes because there's no summit, and it's north of the Whites, so you never touch Kanc traffic.

Peak color: late Sept – ~Oct 8 this year (the north is turning now).

Bear Notch Road, Bartlett → Conway

9 mi of roadLate afternoon light~10% of the Kanc's traffic

The same big views over the Saco valley that people sit in traffic for on the Kancamagus, with three or four pull-offs and almost no cars. This is your scenic drive. It drops you at the east end of the Kanc in Conway; turn left toward Route 16 and you've skipped the whole crowded stretch.

4

Foss Mountain, Eaton

25 min from Conway20 min walk up360° summit, golden hour

A short walk up through wild blueberry fields that turn deep red in October, to a bare summit looking at the Whites in one direction and lakes in the other. Dirt-road access keeps it quiet. Eaton village below has a white church and covered bridge on a small lake for one more frame on the way out.

Peak color: ~Oct 5–12 (mid elevation).

Camera notes
  • Overcast beats sunny for foliage. Colors saturate and you lose harsh contrast.
  • Bring a polarizer. It cuts glare off wet leaves and water, and deepens the sky.
  • Fog over ponds needs a cold, clear, still morning. Check the overnight low; under 40°F is your friend.
  • Underexpose about a third of a stop. Reds blow out before anything else.
  • Shoot the small stuff too: a single maple against spruce, leaves on wet rock, the stone walls on Sandwich Notch Road.

2026 is forecast to run slightly later than usual. Northern NH is turning now through about Oct 8; mid-elevation Whites Oct 5–12; southern NH Oct 12–22. Check a foliage tracker the week before you go.
